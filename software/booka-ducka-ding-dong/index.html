You walk while Booka paces a branching audiobook against route progress. At a decision, you choose with buttons or—in Movement beta—through a sustained change in walking direction. Booka Ducka Ding Dong turns a real walk into a branching audiobook. It plans a return-to-start route, paces scenes against progress, asks meaningful left-or-right questions, remembers the consequences, and saves the finished story and walked route for replay.

01What you can test

  • A free Featured Quest targeting about 40 minutes and defaulting to a return-to-start loop. Finished ElevenLabs duration and real-world route timing are still being validated.
  • Custom AI-generated stories targeting 15–60 minutes. The selected duration is a generation and routing target, not a guaranteed finished runtime.
  • Buttons or Movement beta for choices, with Buttons always available as the manual authority.
  • Adaptive route replacement after a Buttons or Movement choice: the old remaining line and instructions retire, replanning begins from current GPS, and a loop keeps its original finish.
  • Persistent 0.75×, 1×, 1.25×, 1.5×, 1.75×, and 2× audiobook playback speed.
  • Optional on-device procedural ambience while cloud narration loads or an early arrival creates a pacing gap.
  • Local walking-pace learning after three qualifying walks, plus measured timing, pace, and route-update summaries in Library.
  • A persistent audiobook player, lock-screen controls, local Library, replay, and offline audio.
  • Separate story, narration, and cover-art provenance in the Build 6 candidate’s completed-story details.

03The honest route caveat

Booka is an entertainment app, not navigation or a safety service. Movement beta detects a sustained change in walking direction; it does not prove that a particular street is a legal or safe turn. A directional story choice is not a promise that a matching walking route exists. If retries cannot find a reasonable checkpoint, Booka says so and may use a direct route to the unchanged finish. Follow laws, signs, conditions, and your own judgment. Stop and use Buttons whenever looking at or handling the phone would be unsafe.
